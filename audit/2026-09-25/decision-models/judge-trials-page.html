Experiment 01 · decision models as memory judges

A perfect judge would let Smriti send 3× less context and keep 98% of the evidence.

Laya, Jev and CLM-8B are "System One" models: they answer a yes/no or multiple-choice question about a text in one pass, in milliseconds. We are testing whether any of them can re-rank a private memory store well enough to earn a place in it.

Round 3 · official Laya vs hosted Jev · zero-shot · Smriti 0.4.1

Hosted Jev is the only judge that helps out of the box

With the network opened, the real models judged Smriti 0.4.1's top 20 memories. Jev ranks the right memory first 72.5% of the time on held-out questions, against 63.6% for Smriti's own order, and adds 9.2 points of evidence in a 1,500-character context for $0.37 per 1,000 questions. Official Laya, run locally for free, beats a coin flip but not Smriti's ranking yet. Jev's catch: every candidate memory is sent to a hosted API.

Round 2 · trained judge · LME-X held-out test

Trained on our labels, the judge helps, and it showed us a free win

The same Laya-family encoder, frozen, read 10,000 question and memory pairs. A small head trained on 256 questions was judged once on 244 unseen ones. Combined with Smriti's own signals it ranks the right memory first 70.2% of the time, against 63.6%. Its largest learned weight said to demote assistant replies harder; making that one change in Smriti, with no model at all, added up to 5.4 points of evidence reaching the LLM.

Round 1 · Laya-family judge · LME-X dev · 3,000-character context

Round 1 goes to Smriti: the judge was guessing

A community fine-tune of laya-multilingual (MacJev-322M, Apache-2.0) judged Smriti's top 20 memories for 238 questions, zero-shot, on 4 CPU cores. Its scores separate real evidence from the rest at an AUC of 0.47, a coin flip; Smriti's own ranking scores 0.91 on the same questions. Official Laya, Jev and CLM-8B are still to come.

Data table

Data. LoCoMo10 dev conversations (786 questions with evidence labels) and LME-X dev (238 LongMemEval questions, each hidden among 48 other conversations; not the official LongMemEval-S haystack). Tuning happens on dev; model comparisons will be reported on the held-out test split.

Metric. "Evidence reaching the LLM" is the share of labelled supporting turns that appear complete in the packed context. The judge re-ranks Smriti's top 48 turns; with rerank_weight below 1 its score blends with Smriti's own.

Harness. smriti/decision.py speaks the TypeSafe /v1/systemone protocol, so hosted Jev, laya-serve and clm-serve plug in the same way; bench/lab records milliseconds, requests, tokens and dollars per query.
